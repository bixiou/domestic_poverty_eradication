About

This website presents the results of Fabre, A. (2025), “Shortfall of Domestic Resources to Eradicate Extreme Poverty by 2030”, Journal of Development Studies, https://doi.org/10.1080/00220388.2025.2493807, updated with the September 2026 vintage of the World Bank data (2021 PPP). The paper asks whether each low- and middle-income country has enough domestic resources to lift its population above the extreme poverty line, assuming idealized redistribution within the country.

Indicators

Poverty rate
Share of the population living below the poverty line (computed on 100 percentiles: a percentile is poor if its average consumption is below the line).
Poverty gap
Amount needed to lift everyone to the poverty line, i.e. the sum over the poor of the difference between the line and their consumption, expressed in % of the country’s mean consumption (or income).
Antipoverty cap
Income cap such that confiscating all income above it would close the poverty gap. When even confiscating everything above the poverty line is insufficient, the cap is “insufficient” (by convention, it is then set at the mean consumption).
Antipoverty tax
Linear marginal tax rate on income above the taxation threshold needed to close the poverty gap: poverty gap divided by the taxable base. A rate above 100% means that even confiscating all income above the threshold cannot eradicate poverty.
Income floor
Minimum income that could be guaranteed to everyone (by topping up the lowest incomes) with the revenues of a tax at the chosen rate on income above the taxation threshold, recycled within the country.

Method

Each country’s distribution comes from its latest household survey (consumption or income per capita, 100 percentiles). It is projected with balanced growth (all percentiles grow at the same rate as GDP per capita): first with observed GDP per capita growth from the survey year up to 2025 (the last year with GDP data, or the survey year if later), then with the chosen scenario: a uniform rate (3%, 7% or custom), the country’s average growth over 2015–2025, or an autoregressive projection (quadratic model of 2015–25 growth on 1995–2015 growth, applied to 2005–25 growth). Horizons from 2026 to 2100 can be chosen; the growth scenario applies from 2025 to the horizon year (projections to 2050 or 2100 are of course purely illustrative), and world aggregates use the population of the horizon year from the UN World Population Prospects 2022 projections (available for all countries and all horizon years). Countries without GDP data (South Sudan, Venezuela, Yemen) are not grown. The “national accounts” variant scales up survey means to household final consumption expenditure (HFCE) when the latter is higher, attributing the whole gap to the top percentile. World and group aggregates are population-weighted averages of country estimates.

Caveats

  • The policies considered are idealized: perfect targeting, no behavioral responses (e.g. on labor supply or tax evasion), no administrative costs, and no general equilibrium effects. The antipoverty cap and tax are therefore lower bounds on the actual effort, and the income floor an upper bound on what is feasible.
  • Household surveys underestimate top incomes; the HFCE variant gives an upper bound on domestic resources.
  • Projections assume distributionally neutral growth; actual distributions may evolve differently. Surveys are sometimes old (the survey year is shown for each country).
  • With 100 percentiles, poverty rates are precise to about 1 percentage point at country level.
